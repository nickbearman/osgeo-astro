FOSS4G:UK 2026

FOSS4G:UK 2026 – What a Blast!


What a fantastic event! FOSS4G:UK 2026 has come and gone, and we're still buzzing from the experience. It was wonderful to see so many people coming together to share their enthusiasm for open-source geospatial software, exchange ideas, catch up with old friends and make new connections. The sense of community was stronger than ever, with a real hum throughout the event. It's this friendly, welcoming and collaborative atmosphere that makes FOSS4G:UK so special.


The programme certainly didn't disappoint! We were treated to a fantastic variety of presentations, covering an impressive range of topics, technologies and applications. The quality was consistently high, and we'd like to extend a huge thank you to everyone who took the time to prepare and deliver a talk. The workshops were also a great success, giving participants the opportunity to get hands-on, learn new skills and share knowledge. And finally on the topic of presentations we want to thank our keynote speakers, who magically managed to weave a common narrative throughout the three talks, each one building on the previous keynote (how did they do that???). Our three fantastic keynote speakers were:

  • Abigail Page,

    Head of UK Geospatial Strategy at the

    Government Digital Service

  • Fakhar Khalid, Chief Scientist at Sensat

  • Anahid Basiri,

    Chair of Geospatial Data Science

    ,

    School of Geographical & Earth Sciences at the

    University of Glasgow

Of course, none of this would have been possible without our brilliant sponsors, whose generous support helped make the event happen. A massive thank you to all of them! We'd also like to give a special shout-out to the Local Organising Committee, who put in an enormous amount of time and effort behind the scenes. There was plenty of hard work involved, but seeing everything come together so successfully, and everyone having such a great time, made it all worthwhile. The committee couldn't be happier with how it went!

Were you there? We'd love your feedback!

If you attended FOSS4G:UK 2026, please take a few minutes to complete the event feedback form which has been sent out to you. Your comments and suggestions are invaluable in helping us make future events even better.


And if you missed a presentation, or simply want to revisit some of the excellent talks, don't worry! We recorded (most of!) the presentations and will be making the videos available on YouTube as soon as we've had time to process them. Please bear with us while we work through the recordings.


Finally, a huge thank you to everyone who attended, presented, volunteered, sponsored or otherwise contributed to making FOSS4G:UK 2026 such a memorable event. It's the people who make this community what it is, and you've reminded us once again why we love being part of it.

From FOSS4G:UK 2026 to FOSS4G Global 2027 – Let's Keep the Momentum Going!


If FOSS4G:UK 2026 demonstrated anything, it's just how much enthusiasm, talent and energy there is in our open-source geospatial community. And the good news is that we have an even bigger opportunity on the horizon!


The global FOSS4G is coming to Bristol in 2027, bringing the international open-source geospatial community to the UK. Building on the success of this year's event, we have a brilliant opportunity to showcase our community on the world stage and help make FOSS4G 2027 something truly special.


But making an event of this scale happen takes a community effort, and we'd love you to get involved! Whether you can help with organising, volunteering, running workshops, delivering presentations, spreading the word or simply bringing fresh ideas and enthusiasm, there will be plenty of opportunities to contribute. You don't need to be a seasoned conference organiser or an expert developer – just someone who believes in the power of open-source geospatial technology and the community behind it.


FOSS4G:UK 2026 showed what we can achieve when we come together, so let's build on that success and give the global FOSS4G community a fantastic welcome to Bristol in 2027!

OSGeo:UK AGM

OSGeo:UK needs you!

This year’s AGM at FOSS4G:UK 2026 was an important one, with three roles open for election: Secretary and two committee members.


We are pleased to confirm that Ant Scott is continuing on as Secretary (with no other nominations for Secretary). We can also confirm that Juan Ginzo and Steve Karmeinsky have been elected to the OSGeo:UK committee and that their terms will run for three years (2026-2029). We would like to thank everyone who took part and the meeting minutes will be available through the OSGeo:UK website soon. We will be arranging the next Committee Meeting asap, and when we have a date confirmed it will shared on the mailing list. Everyone is welcome to come along, either just to listen, or also contribute if you wish to.

With the changes in the Committee that means there will be a new newsletter author soon, with their own take on this newsletter. Wider News

The 30DayMapChallenge 2026 is coming and will run between Nov 1 to Nov 30, 2026 online. Generate one map a day throughout November, organised together on GitHub under the OSGeo:UK umbrella and be part of two great communities in one fell swoop!


The #30DayMapChallenge is a daily mapping challenge open to everyone. Every November, participants create one map a day for 30 days, each based on a different theme, and share them on social media using the hashtag #30DayMapChallenge.


This year OSGeo:UK is organising maps in a single location using the osgeouk/30DayMapChallenge repository. Everyone is welcome to join in, whether you are a GIS expert or have never made a map before.


Taking part

  • Maps can be prepared in advance, but the spirit of the challenge is to publish one on each themed day.

  • Post your map to Bluesky, Mastodon, LinkedIn, Instagram, X or any platform you prefer with the hashtag. No sign-up is needed.

  • There are no restrictions on the tools, data or technologies you use, and fewer than 30 maps is also fine.

  • For data sources, software and tutorials, see the official Mapping resources page.

Collaborating with OSGeo:UK

Each daily theme has its own GitHub issue, labelled by year.

  1. Pick a day and comment on its issue with ideas, data sources and approaches.

  2. Assign yourself if you want to take the lead on a map, or join someone else’s.

  3. Link or attach the finished map to the issue before publishing it on the themed day.


Find out all the details at https://osgeo.uk/events/30daymapchallenge-2026/
